The Water Balance of Northern Kazakhstan

How a whiteboard sketch grew into field stations, international collaboration and this open geoportal.

National Laboratory Astana · Nazarbayev University
Scroll to begin⌄

Why water balance?

In the semi-arid steppe of Kazakhstan every millimetre of water counts. The project set out to quantify how much water the northern water management basins receive, lose to evaporation and runoff, store and use — and how this has changed over the last 20 years.

It focuses on five water management basins — Zhaiyk-Caspian, Yesil, Tobyl-Torgai, Nura-Sarysu and Ertis — which together cover 69 % of Kazakhstan’s territory, and combines remote sensing, global datasets and national hydrological information.

Chapter 1 · The idea

It started on a whiteboard

The project began as a brainstorming session. The first plan fitted in two lines: (1) review the literature on the available products, the basins and their uncertainties; (2) download the data.

One equation

ΔS = P − E − R − A

Change in water storage equals precipitation minus evapotranspiration, runoff and water abstraction. Each term became a work package — and later a section of this portal.

The hydrological year

The board also fixed the time frame: a water year from 1 October to 1 October, a cold season of snow accumulation and a warm season that starts with snowmelt around April. The sketched basins — split into satellite grid cells and into sub-basins with their river network — show how remote sensing and hydrology were to be combined.

Chapter 3 · Field work

Ground truth for satellites

Satellite products estimate evapotranspiration for whole basins, but they need checking on the ground. The team installed a solar-powered evapotranspiration station with a LI-COR IoE module next to an existing weather station.

Into the steppe

Field campaigns took the team — here Vadim Yapiyev and Nurlan Ongdas — deep into the steppe to choose sites and set up instruments.

Installation

Mast, sensors, solar panel and battery are assembled and wired on site; manuals and toolkits travel on every trip.

Station running

Once powered, the LI-COR logger records evapotranspiration and weather continuously, giving a ground reference for the satellite estimates.

The landscape we measure

Granite outcrops and dry grassland stretching to the horizon — the landscape whose water balance the project describes.

Chapter 6 · Timeseries

The water balance, month by month

The Timeseries dashboard maps precipitation, soil moisture and water balance for every basin. Its Water Balance Products mode compares 27 combinations of precipitation, evapotranspiration and runoff products with GRACE satellite storage for all 14 basins (October 2002 – November 2022).

Chapter 6 · Water Use

Who uses the water?

The Water Use & Abstraction dashboard turns basin statistics for 2004–2023 into maps and charts of withdrawal, use, discharge and losses by sector for the five water management basins. In 2023 they withdrew about 8.8 km³ of water, nearly two thirds of it for industry.
